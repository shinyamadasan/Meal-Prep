## 2. Author frameworks (paraphrased)

### F1. Philosophy: flexible, not rigid

- Healthy eating means **balance, variety and flexibility**. It does not mean deprivation or a
  perfect diet.
- **Convenience foods are legitimate tools.** Examples: frozen rice, canned beans, precut and frozen
  produce, rotisserie chicken, frozen dinners (paired with an added vegetable or protein).
- Low-effort, low-decision meals are explicitly OK. Some recipes need **no cutting at all**.
- The author has ADHD and decision paralysis and dislikes rigid meal prep. The book is designed for
  cooking on days when you have no plan.
- Use an **"add, not subtract"** mindset: improve a meal by adding produce, protein or fiber rather
  than banning foods.
- Food is pleasure as well as nourishment. White rice is fine; whole grains elsewhere in the diet
  make up the fiber gap (p ~161, brown vs white rice).

### F2. Balanced Plate (pp 39–41)

| Portion | What goes in it | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **½ plate** | Produce | Many colors, mostly nonstarchy; fresh, frozen and canned all count |
| **¼ plate** | Protein | Lean; plant-based often |
| **¼ plate** | Carbs | Whole grain preferred; refined is OK in moderation; starchy vegetables count as carb |
| **+ thumb** | Healthy fat | e.g. olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds, sesame oil |

The book's worked examples:
- Sesame Ginger Chicken Wraps
- Chicken Ranch Naan Pizzas
- Blackened Fish Taco Bowls

Each example maps one ingredient to each of the four roles.

### F3. Build-a-Bowl chart (pp 154–55)

A bowl is built from five components: **Base → Protein → Vegetables → Healthy fat → Toppings**.

| Component | Options in the chart |
|---|---|
| Base | Rice (brown/white/wild), quinoa, sweet potato, potato, squash, couscous, corn, barley, sorghum, buckwheat, or leafy greens |
| Protein | Chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, beans/lentils, edamame, pork, shrimp, turkey, beef, canned fish, tofu, tempeh, seitan, cottage cheese |
| Vegetables | Carrots, spinach, broccoli, lettuce, cucumber, zucchini, asparagus, mushrooms, kale, bell pepper, onion, cabbage, eggplant, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts |
| Healthy fat | Cheese, oils, nuts, seeds, avocado, hummus, tahini |
| Toppings | Herbs, pepper flakes, hot sauce, sesame seeds, furikake, toasted nuts/seeds, citrus wedges |

The author's tip: keep a few pre-prepped components in the fridge so a bowl is assembly, not cooking.

### F4. Seven-step meal planning (pp ~53–56)

1. Choose your plan, shop and prep days.
2. Count the meals you will actually eat at home.
3. Pick recipes with **overlapping ingredients**.
4. List the ingredients.
5. **Check what you already have** and cross it off.
6. **Swap and substitute** between recipes to cut the number of distinct items.
7. Shop.

Shopping tips: buy generic brands; don't shop only the store perimeter. The tip list may be
incomplete (see gap 3).

### F5. "A Plan for When You Don't Have a Plan": no-plan grocery template (p ~58–59)

| Role | Count | Rules | Author's examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proteins | **4** | ≥1 low-prep, ≥1 plant-based | chicken breast, eggs, canned tuna, tofu |
| Veggies | **4** | fresh or frozen; many colors; precut for low effort | baby carrots, spring mix, mini peppers, frozen green beans |
| Starches | **3** | ≥2 whole grain; precooked/frozen OK | whole-grain bread, microwave quinoa, frozen white rice |
| Fruits | **3** | fresh or frozen | bananas, apples, frozen blueberries |
| Snacks | **3** | — | pretzels, roasted chickpeas, dark chocolate |
| Flavor enhancers | **3** | — | balsamic vinaigrette, honey mustard, hummus |
| Beverages | **1–2** | minimal added sugar | herbal tea, sparkling water |

This is the core of Required Capability 4. Combined with "check what you already have" (F4 step 5),
the list becomes **what's missing from each role**, not a full shopping list.

### F6. Snack Pack chart (pp 232–33)

A snack is two items from **at least 2 of these 3 groups**:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| High-fiber carb | fruit, vegetables, whole grains, rice cakes, oatmeal |
| Protein | legumes, dairy, animal protein, plant protein, protein shake |
| Healthy fat | nuts, nut butters, seeds, avocado, cheese |

Example pairs: apple + peanut butter; cottage-cheese dip + carrots; crackers + cheese;
yogurt + fruit; popcorn + roasted chickpeas; cereal + milk; oatmeal + walnuts.

### F7. Stocking the kitchen (pp 48–52)

| Area | What to keep |
|---|---|
| Pantry | rice, quick oats, canned beans/lentils, canned vegetables, tomato sauce, microwave rice/quinoa pouches, pasta (incl. chickpea), bread/tortillas/pita, whole-grain crackers, dried fruit, nuts/seeds/butters, canned fish, broth/bouillon, oils, vinegars, spices |
| Flavor builders | reduced-sodium soy, miso, fish sauce, furikake, sesame seeds, chipotles in adobo, tahini, mustard, ketchup, honey/brown sugar, sriracha, gochujang, gochugaru, oyster sauce, hoisin, Thai sweet chili, Thai curry paste, coconut milk |
| Fridge | eggs, milk, cheese, tofu, Greek yogurt, leafy greens, lemons/limes, garlic/onions/scallions (pre-peeled garlic OK) |
| Freezer | frozen vegetables and fruit, frozen rice/quinoa, fish/shellfish, ginger/garlic cubes, edamame, veggie burgers, whole-wheat bread, frozen meals (pair with a vegetable or protein) |

### F8. Protein boosters (Nutrition 101, pp ~20–38)

Ways to add protein to a meal:
- Greek yogurt in smoothies
- egg whites in oats
- protein powder
- seeds or hemp hearts
- cottage cheese in eggs
- beans
- chickpea pasta
- an egg in instant ramen
- rotisserie chicken
- quinoa
- nutritional yeast

Fiber tips: whole grains, produce at every meal, beans/lentils, nuts/seeds, whole fruit over juice.
Hydration guide: about 3.7 L/day for men and 2.7 L/day for women, from all sources.

### F9. Efficiency techniques that recur throughout the book

| Technique | Where it appears | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Sheet pan** | whole chapter (pp ~181–205) | Hands-off, one tray to clean; "an overnight soak" is fine |
| **One pot / one pan** | Italian One-Pot Chicken Pasta | Pasta cooks in the broth; one pot to wash |
| **No-cook assembly** | wraps, salad jars, mason-jar noodles, Five-Minute Tofu | Zero heat, minimal cleanup |
| **Make-ahead sauces and spice blends** | Peanut Sauce (≤1 wk), Jalapeño Cilantro Lime Sauce (≤4 days), blackening blend (jar) | One prep, many meals |
| **Double batch → freeze** | meat sauce, Freeze It! chapter | Thaw overnight or cook from frozen |
| **Freezer-bag "dump" meals** | Freeze It! (pp ~207–227) | Bag raw ingredients, then thaw or dump straight into the pot |
| **Frozen/microwaved vegetables** | Satay Bowls note | Steamed or microwaved mixed vegetables are fine |
| **Cooked rice freezes well** | Freeze It! intro | The rice base is always ready |

### F10. Recipe Remixes and Kitchen Notes

Most recipes end with **Recipe Remix** swaps (protein, vegetable or flavor variations) and
**Kylie's Kitchen Note** (storage, make-ahead, technique). These are the author's *own* sanctioned
adaptations. Record them separately from **our** adaptations (see the Gap Analysis, Recipe
Knowledge section).
