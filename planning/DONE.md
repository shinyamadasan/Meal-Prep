Fixed with `parseTrailingDate()`, which recognises exactly three shapes in trailing position only (`aug 8 2026` / `8 aug 2026` / `2026-08-08`, plus ordinals and `Sept.`); on a match the date is stripped and the remaining text goes through the EXISTING quantity/unit parser — the comma path and `NO_COMMA_RE` are byte-unchanged — and on no match the text is left completely alone. Deliberately NOT an NLP layer: requiring a month WORD (or full ISO) plus a four-digit year is the entire safety argument, and it is what keeps `7 Up`, `Heinz 57 Sauce`, `Formula 1 Protein`, `Vitamin B12`, `12 Grain Bread`, `Omega 3 6 9`, `Vitamin 2000` and `Sauce 12 2026` intact — loosening it to accept a bare trailing number would break all of them at once. `8/8/2026` is refused rather than parsed (day-first in half the world, month-first in the other; a wrong guess moves an expiry by up to eleven months), recognised only so `looksLikeAmbiguousDate()` can warn and name the two unambiguous spellings. Precedence, strongest first: line `exp:` → recognised trailing date → shared field → bought-date + shelf life, with a trailing date stripped even when `exp:` also appears so a typed date can never survive inside the name. `isRealCalendarDate()` round-trips through `Date` and now guards BOTH bulk-add date paths. One expiry model only — records land in the existing `expiryDate`/`dateMode` fields and render through the unchanged D-066 path. Zero new persisted state; no Firestore/`saveData()`/`cloudReady`/tombstone/auth/freshness code touched; `style.css`, `sw.js`, `manifest.json` byte-unchanged.
